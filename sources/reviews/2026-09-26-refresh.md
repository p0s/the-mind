# Source refresh — 26 September 2026

## Outcome

Added 12 previously unlisted sources to `sources/sources.csv`: ten Bach essays, talks, or interviews and two CIMC context updates. The catalog grew from 474 to 486 rows. The public Sources page now links these additions, including the publisher-linked transcripts for the two Artificial Intelligence and You episodes.

The LLM qualia page and its existing claim-ledger entry now distinguish emotional language, functional emotion-related processes, and felt experience. The page also clarifies that fluent self-report is insufficient to establish experience, rather than saying it supplies no evidence. This bounded clarification draws on Bach's AGE essay and Hikari Sorensen's September CIMC note; the core conclusion remains open. No guide or glossary wording changed. The recordings remain candidates for full review; listing them is not evidence of a complete listen or transcript audit.

## Verification scope

- Discovery used broad recent-publication searches plus the official Joscha Bach and CIMC Substack archives. Search snippets and generated search summaries were treated only as leads.
- Titles, authors/channels, dates, and descriptions for seven recordings were checked against their original YouTube publisher metadata. Dates use the UTC calendar day of the publication timestamp; for Future Day and Wes Roth this is the day after the US-local timestamp. Publication and event dates are not interchangeable.
- Both Artificial Intelligence and You episodes were checked against the publisher pages and RSS feed. The feed gives 31 August and 7 September 2026, resolving conflicting search-index dates. A follow-up review read the publisher-linked transcripts selectively: part 1, pages 5–6 and 8–9; part 2, pages 4–6 and 9–10. These passages support retaining the site's caution about implementation and consciousness; they do not constitute a full transcript review.
- Bach's AGE essay and the two CIMC articles were read in full. Publication dates were checked in the publishers' public archive metadata.
- The AAAI proceedings index was inspected for context. Its machine-consciousness section contains multiple authors and positions; the individual papers were not reviewed or imported as Bach sources.
- The existing primary hypothesis paper was retained. This sweep did not compare PDF revisions or re-audit older sources.

## Editorial assessment

- **AGE essay:** useful for the distinction between a task score and multidimensional cognitive agency, and for grounding through interaction. It discusses Rana Gujral's book, human–AI collaboration, and possible futures, rather than replacing the consciousness hypothesis. Keep its forecasts outside the evergreen explanations.
- **CIMC September note:** the researcher note is by Hikari Sorensen. It distinguishes representational resemblance from established phenomenality. Do not attribute the whole institutional update to Bach.
- **CIMC AAAI report:** useful as a route to released research material and as a record of continuing disagreement. Conference participation is not agreement, empirical validation, or endorsement.
- **Audio/video:** descriptions make these relevant candidates for consciousness, self, perception, agency, and AI pages. Any later wording change still needs direct transcript/listening review and a source-specific locator. Davidad's and Sandberg's positions must remain distinguishable from Bach's.
